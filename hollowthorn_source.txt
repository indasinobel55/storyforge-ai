**OONA — The Quiet Lamb**

**AETHELGARD — The Cinder Tiger**

Beneath the curse, he is Aethel, the former Alpha of Garrow's ancestral pack. His true appearance includes a familiar muzzle, a scar over the brow, and a left ear notched by an old battle.

# FINAL PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS

## Narrative continuity
The film must preserve these story facts:
1. Garrow is searching for his scattered old pack when he hears a familiar whimper.
2. The early sighting remains uncertain. Do not reveal that the figure is definitely the lamb Pip or the lost packmate Pip.
3. The Briar Labyrinth traps Garrow and distorts his sense of time.
4. The bell and the scent of chamomile, clover, and warm wool lead him to the Bramble Cloister.
5. Garrow nearly attacks the lambs because of hunger, but their kindness changes his course.
6. Pip is the steady eldest lamb, Bram is the playful and brave smallest lamb, and Oona is the quiet lamb who hums and collects flowers.
7. Garrow gradually heals and learns to trust his new family.
8. Aethelgard attacks the sanctuary and takes Bram. Bram survives and is rescued later.
9. Garrow follows the Cinder Tiger through the labyrinth to the Bone Barrow.
10. The snare cable catches Aethelgard during Garrow's leap.
11. The three lambs reveal their connection to the Weaver-Fleece through their shared song.
12. Silver threads heal Garrow and form a mantle that represents responsibility and connection, not invincibility.
13. Aethelgard is revealed to be Aethel, the former Alpha of Garrow's ancestral pack.
14. The Cannibal Curse is broken by destroying the rune talisman with the Weaver-Fleece's silver magic.
15. Aethel is released from the curse and dissolves into silver-lit ash.
16. The labyrinth heals, the thorn walls open, and the forest receives its first true dawn.
17. Garrow becomes the guardian of his new family without forgetting the family he lost.

## FINAL CREATIVE DIRECTION
This is not simply a story about a wolf rescuing a lamb.
It is a story about a creature who spent his life searching for the family he lost, only to discover that family could still find him in the most unexpected place.

The opening must make the audience feel his loneliness. The cloister must make them feel the warmth he has forgotten. Bram's capture must make them fear losing that warmth. The Bone Barrow must make them believe Garrow is about to lose everything again. The revelation of Aethel must turn the final battle into something deeper than a fight. And the ending must leave the audience with the feeling that grief does not disappear, but love can give it somewhere gentler to rest.

Every frame should serve the story. Every sound and every piece of music should carry emotion. Every eight seconds should feel intentional.
